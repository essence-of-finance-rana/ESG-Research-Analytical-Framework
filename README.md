# ESG Research & Analytical Framework

> A structured research framework examining how sustainability-related issues translate into materiality, risk, strategic response and organizational execution.

---

## Overview

This repository contains a four-part ESG research framework developed to examine sustainability analysis beyond the conventional approach of treating ESG as a collection of disclosures or scores.

The research follows a progression from identifying what matters, to understanding how sustainability-related risks and impacts develop, to examining how organizations can respond and execute.

### Research Architecture

**01 — Financial Materiality**  
Understanding when a sustainability-related issue becomes relevant to enterprise economics, financial performance and investment decision-making.

**02 — Impact & Double Materiality**  
Examining sustainability impacts alongside financial materiality, including the distinction between impact materiality and financial materiality.

**03 — Climate Scenario & Transition Risk**  
Analysing physical and transition risks, scenario architecture, pathways, uncertainty, stress testing and the transmission of climate scenarios into business variables.

**04 — Strategy, Transformation & Execution**  
Examining how sustainability priorities translate into organizational strategy, operating models, capabilities, governance, incentives, KPIs and execution.

---

## The Core Question

The research is structured around a central question:

> **How does an ESG or sustainability-related issue move from being an abstract sustainability concern to something that can influence business risk, financial outcomes and strategic decision-making?**

The framework therefore moves through four analytical layers:

**Materiality → Risk & Impact → Strategic Response → Execution**

---

## Research Approach

The project combines:

- Primary institutional and regulatory sources
- Professional investment-research guidance
- Academic literature
- Climate and sustainability frameworks
- Company-level examples and evidence
- Explicit analytical synthesis

A deliberate distinction is maintained between:

**Established / sourced frameworks**  
and  
**Analytical synthesis developed within the research.**

Where a framework or methodology is not directly prescribed by an authoritative source, it is identified as analytical synthesis rather than presented as an official standard or methodology.

---

## Dossier 01 — Financial Materiality

**Focus:** When does a sustainability-related issue become financially relevant?

The first dossier establishes the foundation for analysing sustainability through a financial lens, examining materiality, decision-useful information, transmission mechanisms and the distinction between ESG information and financial materiality.

### Key themes

- Materiality assessment
- Financial relevance
- ESG information and investment analysis
- Transmission from sustainability issue to financial effect
- Magnitude and significance
- Valuation implications
- ESG scores versus material information

---

## Dossier 02 — Impact & Double Materiality

**Focus:** How should financial relevance and sustainability impact be considered together?

The second dossier develops the distinction between **financial materiality** and **impact materiality**, examining how a sustainability issue can matter because of its effects on enterprise prospects, because of the organization's impacts on people and the environment, or both.

### Key themes

- Impact materiality
- Financial materiality
- Double materiality
- Severity and likelihood
- Stakeholder and value-chain considerations
- Materiality assessment
- ESG performance versus investment attractiveness

---

## Dossier 03 — Climate Scenario & Transition Risk

**Focus:** How can climate scenarios be translated into business-relevant risk analysis?

The third dossier examines the architecture of climate scenario analysis and the distinction between scenarios, pathways, models and company forecasts.

It also examines physical and transition risks, compounding and cascading risks, scenario testing, reverse stress testing and vulnerability mapping.

### Key themes

- Physical risk
- Transition risk
- Climate scenarios and pathways
- NGFS scenario architecture
- IEA scenario frameworks
- Scenario uncertainty
- Scenario-to-business transmission
- Stress testing
- Reverse stress testing
- Vulnerability mapping

---

## Dossier 04 — Strategy, Transformation & Execution

**Focus:** What happens after a sustainability issue has been identified?

The fourth dossier moves from diagnosis toward organizational response, examining how sustainability priorities can translate into strategy, operating models, capabilities, governance, incentives, KPIs and execution.

### Key themes

- Sustainability strategy
- Organizational transformation
- Governance
- Operating-model implications
- Capabilities
- Incentives
- KPIs and performance management
- Capital allocation
- Execution

---

# Applied Analysis

Alongside the four research dossiers, the project includes an **ESG benchmarking analysis covering 722 companies across 45 industry groups**.

The analysis is designed to examine ESG performance in an industry-relative context rather than treating a single aggregate ESG score as universally comparable across companies and sectors.

### Analytical coverage

- Environmental performance
- Social performance
- Governance performance
- Industry-level benchmarking
- Company-level comparison
- Relative ESG positioning

The accompanying Excel workbook contains the applied analysis and supporting calculations.
